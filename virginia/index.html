How to get a Virginia home care organization licence in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Va. Code §§ 32.1-162.7–162.15 and 12VAC5-381 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Virginia, you need a Home Care Organization (HCO) Licence from the Virginia Department of Health — Office of Licensure and Certification before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as a home care organization — the licence requirement in the regulation's own gate sentence, the sanction bar, branch offices, the organization name, non-transferability, display of the licence, and the consequences of unlicensed operation and advertising stated soberly; the exemptions said plainly — who does not need this licence at all, the homemaker/chore/companion definitions quoted, the post-licensure certification exemption and the $75 lane; the personal-care lane on the -50 compliance matrix with the verbatim -300 skilled list as the refer-out boundary and the § 54.1-3408 medication line; application, renewal and fees — the OLC Portal, the 60-day pre-opening filing, the $1,500 fee, the three-month budget and reserve proofs, the six-month inactivity rule, prelicensure consultation, the triennial renewal and the licence-in-effect rule; changes, reissuance, closure and return of the licence with the 30-working-day and 30-day clocks kept apart; unannounced at-least-biennial inspections, home visits on voluntary consent, and the plan of correction on the 15- and 45-working-day clocks; OLC complaint investigations and variances; enforcement — revocation, suspension, cease-operations, reinstatement, the DMAS notification and the insurance-lapse revocation warning; the governing body and the employee-administrator with the designee and availability rules; and management, business operations and financial controls — posted hours, truthful advertising, the written-policy system, GAAP records, the triennial CPA audit and documented anti-theft controls.

The numbers the rule actually sets

The $1,500 triennial fee, the 60-day pre-opening filing, the 3-month budget and reserve proofs, the CCRE 30- and 90-day clocks, $1M and $50,000 insurance minimums with lapse meaning revocation, 90-day supervision, and the 15/45-working-day plan-of-correction clocks — exactly as the regulation states them.

What is moving in Virginia right now

Virginia amended the chapter effective 22 May 2025 — the licence went TRIENNIAL and the fee to $1,500 for three years — and this edition is written to that amended text throughout. The ground that could move is tracked honestly: the full-chapter rewrite (Town Hall action 5352) was returned to the agency in June 2026 and is NOT in force — no sentence of this manual rests on it; SB 241, with its home-office ban, was continued to the 2027 session and is NOT law. Part D carries each watch with what to do, and every text this edition could not capture is graded "summary — verify", never passed off as read.

The timeline

  1. Decide the lane — personal care only — and answer the exemption question first: providers of ONLY homemaker, chore or companion services are exempt and do not need this licence at all
  2. Form the entity and the governing body, noting the sanction bar — no licence issues to a person sanctioned under 42 USC § 1320a-7b — and that state licensure comes FIRST, before any federal certification or national accreditation
  3. Appoint the qualified administrator — an employee, designated in writing, with one year of direct health care service delivery and a supervisory or administrative year within the last five — and designate the supervising registered nurse for personal care services
  4. Bind the three insurance lines before opening — malpractice per § 8.01-581.15, general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence, and third-party crime insurance or a fidelity bond of at least $50,000 — remembering the statute's own sanction: a lapse is revocation, not a fine
  5. Build the money proofs — the detailed operating budget for the three months after licensure, and proof of reserve operating funds for the same three months by current balance sheet, bank-officer letter or letter of credit
  6. Create the OLC Portal account and file the completed application with the $1,500 non-refundable fee AT LEAST 60 days before the planned opening date
  7. Answer every incompleteness notice promptly — an incomplete application becomes inactive six months after receipt, and the applicant reapplies with a new fee
  8. Take the prelicensure consultation — the OLC arranges it only after a completed initial application is on file — and bring the required-policy index
  9. Stand ready for the applicant inspection: the OLC may inspect to verify application information, and the commissioner issues the licence on finding compliance with the law and the regulation
  10. Day one — post the licence for public inspection, keep regular posted business hours with services provided or arranged on-call 24 hours a day, and run the screening machinery on every hire from the first
  11. Hold the inspection posture permanently — unannounced on-site inspections come at least every two years, the acceptable plan of correction is due within 15 working days of the inspection report, and corrective actions complete within 45 working days
  12. Arm the triennial renewal the day the licence issues — the third-anniversary expiry, renewal forms available at least 60 days before it, the $50 late-filing fee, and the licence-in-effect rule protecting a timely complete filing

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The lane confirmed — personal care only — and the exemption question answered and documented: providers of only homemaker, chore or companion services do not need this licence at all
  • All six option switches set with the alternatives deleted, and every bracketed field filled
  • The entity formed and the governing body constituted, with its duties and approvals minuted
  • The administrator appointed in writing — an employee, with one year of direct health care service delivery and a supervisory or administrative year within the last five
  • The supervising registered nurse designated for personal care services, with an RN or LPN available during all hours services are provided
  • The three insurance lines bound — malpractice per § 8.01-581.15, general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence, and crime or fidelity cover of at least $50,000 — knowing a lapse is revocation, not a fine
  • The three-month operating budget prepared and the three-month reserve proof in hand — current balance sheet, bank-officer letter or letter of credit
  • The OLC Portal account created and the completed application filed with the $1,500 non-refundable fee at least 60 days before the planned opening date
  • Every incompleteness notice answered promptly — an incomplete application becomes inactive six months after receipt and costs a new fee
  • Every 12VAC5-381-180 policy adopted by the governing body and dated, mapped to the regulation's own 35-item catalogue
  • The screening machinery ready: the sworn disclosure statement from every applicant, and the original CCRE criminal record report within 30 days of employment and no older than 90 days at hire, kept in a locked file
  • No unsupervised direct client contact before the original criminal record report is received
  • The prelicensure consultation taken once the completed application is on file
  • The licence posted for public inspection, business hours posted, and the 24/7 on-call arrangement live
  • The emergency operations plan prepared, with the current list of clients requiring specialized assistance
  • The pre-service disclosure set ready — services, fees, payment sources and notice terms — and the client-rights review documented at every admission
  • The plan of services written by the registered nurse with its six required elements and reviewed with the assigned home attendant before services begin
  • The inspection posture standing — unannounced visits come at least every two years, the record production index current, and the 15-working-day plan-of-correction clock understood
  • Supervisory contacts cycling at not more than 90 days, and 12 hours of annual in-service tracking per home attendant
  • The triennial renewal armed on day one — the third-anniversary expiry diarised with the $50 late fee in view — and the watch items logged: the pending chapter rewrite and SB 241's successor
