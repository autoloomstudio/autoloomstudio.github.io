How to get a New York licensed home care services agency licence in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to PHL Article 36 and 10 NYCRR Parts 765–766 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in New York, you need a Licensed Home Care Services Agency (LHCSA) Licence from the New York State Department of Health — Public Health and Health Planning Council approval before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as a licensed home care services agency — who must be licensed under the 765-2.1(d) accountability criteria, the exemptions, PHHPC approval, the NYSE-CON application with the $2,000 fee and its 30-day cheque clock, the public-need, financial-feasibility and character-and-competence reviews, and the post-approval sequence in which this manual is itself submitted to and accepted by the Home Care regional office before the pre-opening survey; the scope of licensed services and the licence face, with the 766.2(b) service set, the statutory definitions and the plain statement that the Agency is not a certified home health agency; the governing authority's responsibility for management, legal compliance and policy adoption; public information; offices and agency records with the 766.12 record sets, retention classes and electronic controls; management contracts and franchise agreements where used, with the non-delegable elements of control; Health Commerce System accounts, the communications directory on its monthly minimum and the 24/7 emergency contacts; licence amendments and the ALA process as revised by DAL 26-02, with the 90/90/30/10-day clocks and the rule that ownership and control changes always go to the PHHPC; annual registration under PHL 3605-b and the UDCS statistical report; and closure, surrender and enforcement with the 30-day notice, the continuation-of-care and records plans, hearing rights and Article 78 review.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$2,000 NYSE-CON fee with the 30-day cheque clock, the five-LHCSA no-need presumption, two months' working capital, the 30-day information-response clock, the six-month plan-of-care review, the 10-business-day registry entry, the $500-per-month unregistered-operation fine, the 90/90/30/10-day amendment clocks — exactly as the texts state them.

What is moving in New York right now

No other state in this series makes the policy and procedure manual itself a licensure gate: after PHHPC contingent approval it goes to the Home Care regional office, and the pre-opening survey is scheduled only after it is accepted. And New York's home care law is moving — DAL 26-02 redrew the amendment lanes in January 2026, the limited-licence scheme sunsets June 30, 2027, the advanced home health aide provisions carry a March 2029 repeal note, and the wage parity calendar and regional aide minimum wage move every year. Part D tracks each one, and every text this edition could not capture is graded "summary — verify", never passed off as read.

The timeline

  1. Choose the lane: de novo, change of ownership, or ALP/PACE/NFP/CCRC-affiliated — ownership and control changes always go to the PHHPC, never the administrative lane
  2. Read the county no-need report and set the county strategy: five or more LHCSAs actively serving a county raises the rebuttable presumption of no need, and any rebuttal is data-driven evidence built from the 765-1.16(d) factors with your own local data
  3. Form the entity and prepare the 765-1.2(c) attachments: assumed-name certificate, partnership agreements with the prescribed PHHPC-approval language verbatim, controlling-person documents, agent-for-service designation
  4. Assemble CPA-supported financials to the two-months working-capital standard, with start-up funding shown and projections in which revenues meet expenditures over time
  5. Prepare the character-and-competence disclosures: every health, adult care or mental health facility operated, with dates, and for any penalty or suspension the violation, the enforcing body, the remediation and the restoration
  6. Get on the Health Commerce System through CAMU with the Director/Administrator role — NYSE-CON, the statistical report and the communications directory all run on it
  7. File in NYSE-CON and send the $2,000 cheque to the Bureau of Project Management within 30 days of submission, the submission number on its face — non-refundable once acknowledged
  8. Answer every information request within 30 days (failure is deemed abandonment and withdrawal) and wait on the PHHPC's bi-monthly calendar — no overall processing time is published and none is printed here
  9. On contingent approval, complete and self-audit the policy and procedure manual and submit it with the required documents to the Home Care regional office; action the reviewer's comments
  10. After the manual is accepted, prepare for the pre-opening survey from the production index
  11. Licence in hand, load the four operating clocks: annual registration (a $500 fine for each month, or part of a month, unregistered), the annual statistical report through UDCS, the wage parity calendar where it applies, and the amendment clocks — 90 days for service adds and name changes, 30 for other-service notices, 10 for address changes, 30 for closure

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The application lane chosen — de novo, change of ownership, or ALP/PACE/NFP/CCRC-affiliated — knowing ownership and control changes always go to the PHHPC
  • The county no-need report pulled for every county you will name: five or more LHCSAs actively serving raises the rebuttable presumption of no need, and any rebuttal built as data-driven evidence from the 765-1.16(d) factors — demographics, documented waiting lists, existing agencies' capacity and quality, workforce, cultural competency, specialty subpopulations
  • The access commitment understood: approval on need carries the agreement to serve population groups with access difficulty due to minority status, age, medical history, case complexity, payment source or geographic location
  • The entity formed, with the 765-1.2(c) attachments assembled: assumed-name certificate, partnership agreements carrying the prescribed PHHPC language verbatim, controlling-person and parent-corporation documents, agent-for-service designation
  • CPA-supported financials to the two-months working-capital standard, with start-up funding shown and projections in which revenues meet expenditures over time
  • The character-and-competence disclosures complete: every health, adult care or mental health facility operated, with dates — and for any penalty or suspension, the violation, the enforcing body, the remediation and whether it was restored
  • Schedule 2D compliance reports in hand for any out-of-state affiliated facilities
  • Workers' compensation and disability/paid-family-leave coverage proofs ready
  • Health Commerce System accounts live through CAMU with the Director/Administrator role — NYSE-CON, the statistical report and the communications directory all run on it
  • The $2,000 application fee ready as a cheque to the New York State Department of Health, reaching the Bureau of Project Management within 30 days of submission with the NYSE-CON submission number on its face — non-refundable once acknowledged
  • Every information request answered within 30 days during review — failure is deemed abandonment and withdrawal — and every verification authorization signed
  • The policy and procedure manual completed to the Department's review elements — agency name exact throughout, every policy numbered, dated, cited and cross-referenced, a job description and policy set for each service on the licence face — and self-audited before it goes to the regional office
  • The required submission documents assembled with the manual: tax identification letter, emergency preparedness plan, admission packet, agency brochure, patient bill of rights, QI committee roster, clinical and personnel record forms
  • Personnel machinery ready to run: identity verified before hire, the Criminal History Record Check process with outcomes recorded in fixed vocabulary only, the Home Care Registry pre-service check and 10-business-day entry, health assessments, rubella and measles and TB records, orientation before patient contact
  • The clinical spine adopted: the RN safe-and-adequate acceptance decision, the professional assessment and a plan of care reviewed no less frequently than every six months, medical orders with their authentication clocks, clinical supervision, the full patient care record
  • The twelve patient rights ready to be given in writing before care begins, with the complaint procedure and its 15-day response
  • The pre-opening survey posture set once the manual is accepted: office record set in place, personnel files complete, clinical forms adopted, governing authority minutes current
  • The four operating clocks loaded before day one: annual registration (a $500 fine for each month, or part, unregistered), the annual statistical report through UDCS, the wage parity calendar where it applies, and the 90/90/30/10-day amendment clocks
  • The communications directory current on its monthly minimum, with 24-hour emergency contacts designated
  • The watch items diarised: DAL 26-02's amendment lanes, the limited-licence sunset of June 30, 2027, the advanced home health aide repeal note of March 31, 2029, the current year's wage figures, each cycle's statistical-report deadline
