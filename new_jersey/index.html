How to get a New Jersey health care service firm registration in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to N.J.A.C. 13:45B-13 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in New Jersey, you need a Health Care Service Firm (HCSF) Registration from the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Registration as a health care service firm — the application, the bond, the fee, the July 1 renewal and the changes that must be reported; scope of services and the boundary with home health agencies; ownership, officers, the managing agent and the crime disclosure; the administrator and the registered professional nurse; accreditation within 12 months and its maintenance; policy adoption and compliance monitoring; business premises and primary locations; financial solvency, the bond, workers' compensation, insurance and the annual financial statement; advertising; Division investigations, hearings, enforcement and penalties; client records and retention; change of ownership, closure and continuity of care.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$500 a year, $25 late fee, July 1 expiry, $10,000 bond, 12 months to accreditation, 30 days to report changes, 24-hour disclosures, 2-year waiver file, 76 training hours, 2-year certificate, 60 + 60 days conditional employment, $50 gift threshold, $2,000 / $5,000 penalties, $500 a day unregistered — exactly as the rules state them.

What is moving in New Jersey right now

This manual applies N.J.A.C. 13:45B-13.5A as amended in June 2025, the 2022 gift-and-power-of-attorney rule and the amended N.J.S.A. 34:8-44, and it says plainly which standards come from the accreditor rather than the rule. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when the Division, the Board, the Legislature or your accrediting body moves.

The timeline

  1. Set up: register the entity with the New Jersey Division of Revenue and obtain the IRS Employer Identification Number; secure a primary location that is not a residence, or has separate ingress and egress, or where no client or job seeker is ever met; identify every officer, director, principal, 10 % owner and managing agent and collect each crime certification; arrange workers' compensation insurance — it is part of the definition of a health care service firm — and [general and professional liability — Agency standard; no rule sets an insurance minimum beyond workers' compensation]; arrange the $10,000 bond or the net-worth waiver; adopt your policy manual and complete;; decide COMPANION-ONLY, MEDICATION-DELEGATION, BOND, COMPANION BACKGROUND CHECK and the supervisory visit interval. Do not recruit, advertise, offer services or sign a contract before the registration issues (§ 13:45B-13.6(a)1).
  2. Register with the Regulated Business Section on the Division's form with the attachments in C3 and the $500.00 fee per primary location; answer any request promptly; a refusal carries a 30-day hearing request.
  3. Registration issued: display it [verify whether the Division requires posting]; enter the July 1 expiry, the 12-month accreditation deadline and the renewal dates; apply to the accrediting body at once.
  4. Staff: appoint the administrator and the registered professional nurse; recruit CHHAs and verify every certificate before the first shift; issue employment letters for any candidate still to be certified; obtain the Board's background-check clearance or run conditional employment on the sworn statement, applying the 60-or-older supervision restriction only if the aide contests a disqualification; orient, test and have the RN verify competency before any task is delegated; issue identification tags; recruit companions with the employment-history or two-reference verification and the work-authorisation certification, the Agency-standard background check and the orientation course.
  5. First clients: intake and service-category determination; the Consumer Guide to every client and, for every companion, the uncertified-worker disclosure, each at least 24 hours before the first service or with a written waiver kept two years; the service agreement; for CHHA clients the RN assessment and the nursing plan of care reviewed with the CHHA; for companion clients the companion service plan; the rights notice; daily documentation from day one; the first supervisory visit within [the first two visits] of a new CHHA assignment and thereafter at least every [60] days.
  6. Within 12 months of registration: accreditation obtained; if it lapses later, notify the Division in writing within 10 days.
  7. Every year: renew on or prior to July 1 with the renewal application, the bond, the $500.00 fee per location, the accreditation evidence and, from 1 July 2026, the annual financial statement — a late renewal costs $25.00 more; report any ownership or management change before it happens and any other change within 30 calendar days; re-check every CHHA certificate at its two-year renewal and the two-year follow-up background check; run the in-service programme, the annual evaluations, the emergency plan review and the quality self-audit; re-check the rule.
  8. Public programmes: after registration and accreditation you may seek enrolment as a Medicaid Personal Care Assistance provider or with managed care organisations or Veterans Affairs; those programmes set their own requirements (including the 12 in-service hours a year in N.J.A.C. 10:60-1.2 and the § 13:45B-13.5A Medicaid audit thresholds) — keep their policies in a separate binder.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity registered with the New Jersey Division of Revenue; IRS Employer Identification Number; a primary location that is not a residence, or has separate ingress and egress, or where no client or job seeker is ever met (§ 13:45B-3.1(c)1); telephone answered during stated hours
  • Every officer, director, principal, 10 % owner and managing agent listed with residence and business addresses and the crime certification signed; the 30-day update log opened
  • Workers' compensation insurance in force; the $10,000 compliance bond or the net-worth waiver with the CPA report ready; [liability insurance — Agency standard]
  • Nothing advertised, no staff recruited, no services offered and no contract signed before the registration issues; the advertising log opened for the day after
  • The registration application complete with every § 13:45B-13.3(a) item and the $500.00 fee per primary location; the hearing-request deadline understood
  • The accrediting body chosen [CAHC / CHAP — verify], its standards obtained, the application ready to file on registration, the 12-month deadline on the calendar
  • Administrator and registered professional nurse appointed with the New Jersey RN licence verified; the option switches chosen and written into
  • At least two complete personnel files — one CHHA (certificate verified, background check — or the conditional-employment sworn statement with the supervision-restriction log only if a disqualification is contested — employment letter, competency checklist signed by the RN, ID tag issued) and one companion (employment history or two references verified, work authorisation certified, orientation course named, Agency background check)
  • Client packet ready: the current Consumer Guide to Homemaker-Home Health Aides [edition — verify] with the receipt and waiver form; the uncertified-worker disclosure on letterhead with all four items; the service agreement with the registration number; the rights notice with the Division's address and telephone and the Board's contact
  • RN assessment and nursing plan of care forms with the CHHA review record and the medication delegation record; companion service plan; daily documentation sheets; medication administration record; supervisory visit record
  • Gift and financial-relationship declaration file opened; every CHHA and companion has signed the no-power-of-attorney, no-loans attestation
  • Adult Protective Services county contacts for every county served entered with [1-800-792-8820 — verify] and 9-1-1; every employee oriented to the reporting duty
  • Emergency preparedness and continuity plan complete; infection control programme and TB screening in place; incident and complaint logs opened; quality improvement plan and self-audit scheduled
  • Client record system and retention schedule in place; document map completed; everything producible to the Division and the accreditor
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a registered health care service firm providing companion services [and certified homemaker-home health aide services], carry the registered name and address, never imply home health agency licensure, and appear only after registration (§ 13:45B-15.1; § 13.6(a)1)
