How to get a Pennsylvania home care agency license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Pennsylvania, you need a Home Care Agency License from the Pennsylvania Department of Health before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Governing body and administrator, scope and service area, licensure and notices, inspections and plans of correction, policy development, quality, nondiscrimination, records and retention, exception requests, fees and consumer funds, compliance with other laws.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$100 fee, 60-day renewal, two satisfactory references, 1-year criminal history report, 30/90-day provisional hiring, 12-month TB, annual competency review, 10-day termination notice — all exactly as the rule states them.

What is moving in Pennsylvania right now

This manual works the way the Department works now: the online portal, the exception request for video interviews, the current Consumer Notice of Direct Care Worker Status. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when Chapter 611 or the Department's forms move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: register the business and any fictitious name with the Department of State; secure the physical office; obtain insurance; adopt your policy manual; obtain criminal history reports for every owner and the Administrator; complete the Administrator's TB screening if he or she will visit consumers; build the consumer packet.
  2. Apply through the portal with the $100 fee and every form. Processing times are not published; the Department's change-of-ownership page says review of a CHOW "can take up to a minimum of 30 days" — plan for longer for an initial application.
  3. Pre-licensure inspection at your office. Deficiencies produce a report of inspection; submit a written plan of correction within 30 days of receipt or sooner if directed (35 P.S. §448.814(a);).
  4. Licence issued for one year, expiring on the last day of the month of issue (35 P.S. §448.809(a)(1)(i)). Post it conspicuously (§448.809(b)). Do not sign a service agreement or provide services before it issues (§611.2(a)).
  5. After licensure: the Department may inspect at any time during regular business hours and inspects before each renewal; submit the renewal form and fee at least 60 days before expiry (§611.2(e);); send every Chapter 51 and Department notice when things change; keep the Department's contact information for your agency current (the Department's announcements ask for it).
  6. Medicaid: after licensure you may apply to the Office of Long-Term Living to become a waiver provider (FAQ: 1-800-932-0939 option 1; RA-HCBSEnProv@pa.gov); programme standards then add to Chapter 611.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Physical office at the application address, open during stated business hours; a conspicuous place chosen for the licence; business registration and fictitious name certificate in the licence file
  • Current copy of 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611 at the office (§611.4(a)); Chapter 51 and the Act available
  • Governing Body resolution adopting the manual and appointing the Administrator; organisational chart; job descriptions signed; Password Agreement signed
  • Nondiscrimination policy adopted, dated, posted; staff notification signed; publicity planned
  • Owners' criminal history reports on file and available (§611.52(a), (f)); ChildLine verifications if serving minors, or the adults-only attestation
  • Administrator's and office staff's criminal history reports (within the past year) and TB documentation where there will be direct consumer contact
  • At least one complete direct care worker file : interview date, two satisfactory references, criminal history report with request form and residency declaration, competency documentation, TB screening dated within one year, job description, acknowledgment, orientation
  • Competency examination with answer key, and the training programme curriculum covering all sixteen §611.55 subject areas, on file
  • Consumer information packet assembled; the service agreement reviewed for the 10-day and 14-day arrears terms
  • Consumer record system ready; home folder set; complaint, incident and Department-notification forms ready
  • Calendar started: licence expiry and 60-day renewal date; TB and competency due dates; publicity due date
  • Emergency preparedness plan completed; infection control supplies on hand; home safety assessment form ready
  • Insurance in place and disclosed correctly (liability — maintains / does not maintain; workers' compensation)
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a licensed home care agency providing non-medical services and claim no licence before it issues
