How to get a Texas home care agency license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to 26 TAC Chapter 558 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Texas, you need a Home Care Agency (HCSSA) License from the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

The HCSSA licence in the personal assistance services category — term, fees, renewal and display; scope of services and the licensing boundary; the licence holder, organizational structure and lines of authority; the administrator and alternate administrator and their training; policy adoption, review and compliance monitoring; changes, notices, operating hours, closure and voluntary suspension; surveys, initial survey readiness, plans of correction and informal dispute resolution; enforcement, penalties and appeals; client records, the client list, release and retention; financial solvency, business records, billing and the prohibition of solicitation; independent contractors and branch offices; dissolution and the client transfer plan.

The numbers the rule actually sets

3-year license, $2,625, renewal by day 45, 45 + 45 processing days, 8 / 24 / 12 training hours, 6 months to readiness, 3 home visits, 10 days for a plan of correction, 12-month registry re-checks, 24 hours to report abuse, 14 working days to file notes, 5-day discharge notice, 5-year records — exactly as the rule states them.

What is moving in Texas right now

This manual applies Chapter 558 as it stands — the 2021 rewrite, the 2023 emergency preparedness amendment, the Employee Misconduct Registry rule's 2022 move to Chapter 561 — and flags every point that HHSC's own pages, the 2025 session or the Texas Register could change: the portal, the fees, the Presurvey Training, the penalty tables. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when they move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: register the entity with the Texas Secretary of State and obtain the IRS Employer Identification Number; secure a place of business in Texas (§ 558.11(e)) with secure record storage — original active records must be kept there (§ 558.301(a)(4)); identify every owner of 5 % or more, controlling person, affiliate and the chief financial officer and confirm none has a 26 TAC Chapter 560 conviction; designate the administrator and alternate in writing and have each complete eight clock hours of qualifying training and HHSC's Presurvey Training; adopt your policy manual by resolution and complete;, and; decide DRUG-TESTING, DELEGATION and PEDIATRIC; obtain insurance [general and professional liability, workers' compensation or an occupational accident policy, non-owned auto — Agency standard; Texas sets no insurance minimum for a HCSSA in the sources read]; recruit, screen, orient, test and evaluate at least one attendant so that two complete personnel files exist; build the client packet. Do not admit a client (§ 558.11(a)), and do not advertise personal assistance services (§ 558.404(b)), before the licence issues.
  2. Apply in the online portal on Form 2021 for the personal assistance services category with the attachments in C3 and the $2,625 fee; answer any deficiency notice within 30 days; expect up to 45 + 45 days.
  3. Licence issued: display it (§ 558.211); enter the expiry date, the renewal deadline (the 45th day before expiry), the six-month readiness deadline and the survey cycle; enrol the administrator and alternate in HHSC's emergency communication system (§ 558.256(q)); confirm your listing on HHSC's provider search [verify].
  4. First clients: intake and admission record; the supervisor's on-site visit and determination of services; the agreement, consent, rights notice, complaint statement, advance-directive notice, drug-testing policy, emergency information, ANE policy and — for clients 60 and over — the Rights of the Elderly, all acknowledged; the individualized service plan signed by the client or family and the Agency before services start; the backup designee agreement; daily notes from day one, filed within 14 working days; the client list updated; the first supervisory visit within [60] days.
  5. Within six months of the licence: at least one client admitted and served; HHSC Form 2020 Notification of Readiness for Initial Survey submitted to the designated survey office with each client's name, admission date and category; the four § 558.521(d) documents ready; the administrator's remaining sixteen training hours completed within twelve months of designation.
  6. The initial survey (unannounced; at least three home visits); plan of correction within 10 days; corrections within 2, 7, 20 or 60 days by severity; IDR within 10 days if you dispute a finding.
  7. After licensure: the next survey within 18 months, then at least every 36 months; the QAPI committee twice a year and the plan reviewed yearly; the emergency plan reviewed and drilled yearly; registry re-checks every 12 months; 12 hours of administrator continuing education in each 12-month period; every change reported within 7 or 30 days with the $30 fee; the renewal application, posted in the portal 120 days before expiry, submitted with the $2,625 fee no later than the 45th day before expiry; client records kept five years after discharge; re-check the rule at each annual review.
  8. Public programmes: after licensure you may seek a Medicaid provider agreement (for example Medicaid personal attendant programmes administered by HHSC or its managed care organisations [programme names — verify]), a Veterans Affairs community care contract or an Area Agency on Aging contract — each of which imposes requirements your policy manual does not contain, such as electronic visit verification, client-managed attendant care under § 558.404(g)(3) and RN assessment rules (see the Scope Note); those programmes set their own requirements — keep their policies in a separate binder.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity registered with the Texas Secretary of State; IRS Employer Identification Number; a place of business in Texas with secure storage for original active client records and a telephone answered during stated operating hours
  • Licence holder identified; owners of 5 % or more, controlling persons, affiliates and the chief financial officer listed and clear of Chapter 560 convictions; resolutions passed adopting your policy manual; organizational chart with lines of authority to the client care level
  • Administrator and alternate administrator designated in writing, each meeting § 558.244, with the eight pre-designation training hours and HHSC's Presurvey Training recorded; the employee who can admit surveyors named in writing; operating hours fixed and posted if not 8–5 Monday–Friday
  • Every policy Chapter 558 requires in writing adopted and listed; the drug testing choice, the delegation choice and the pediatric choice made and written into, and; the signed compliance statement collected from every direct care worker
  • At least two complete personnel files : Chapter 250 check dated before first contact; printed NAR and EMR results; EMR written information acknowledged within five working days of hire; signed job description; orientation; competency checklist signed before the first assignment; written test; TB screening (Agency standard); training record
  • Supervisor designated with the § 558.404(g)(2) qualification recorded; the supervision policy written with the default interval and the client/family-input step
  • Client packet ready: agreement with every § 558.292 item and the schedule of charges; informed consent; rights notice with the eight rights, the HHSC Complaint and Incident Intake statement (P.O. Box 149030, Austin, Texas 78714-9030, 1-800-458-9858) and the Rights of the Elderly; advance directives notice; drug testing policy statement; emergency information with STEAR and 2-1-1; ANE policy summary with the DFPS and HHSC numbers
  • Service plan form with the six § 558.404(f)(2) elements and the plan of supervision; on-site visit and functional assessment form; daily documentation sheets; backup designee agreement
  • Emergency preparedness and response plan complete — risk assessment, coordinators, continuity of operations, communication, triage categories, STEAR, client information, staff training, drill and review dates; administrator and alternate enrolled in HHSC's emergency communication system
  • Dissolution and client transfer plan written; financial resources evidence assembled; business records and billing policy in place
  • QAPI committee of three named and the written plan adopted with the first two meeting dates set; client satisfaction survey ready; infection log opened; incident and complaint logs opened
  • Client list form ready for the first admission; client record checklist and retention log ready; Form 2020 readiness worksheet and the six-month deadline on the calendar
  • Document map completed; everything producible within eight working hours
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a licensed home and community support services agency providing personal assistance services, and appear only after the licence issues (§ 558.404(b))
