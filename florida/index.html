How to get a Florida homemaker and companion services provider registration in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to s. 400.509, Fla. Stat., ch. 408 Part II, Rule 59A-8.025 and ch. 59A-35 F.A.C. · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Florida, you need a Homemaker and Companion Services (HCS) Provider Registration from the Florida Agency for Health Care Administration — Long Term Care Services Unit before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Registration as a homemaker and companion services provider — who must register (including sole proprietors working alone), the exemptions, Form 3110-1003 (July 2024) and the Addendum, the $50.75 fee, the AHCA file number, completeness as documents plus fees plus screening results, the 30/21/60-day review clocks, no initial inspection and the two-year term; the scope of registered services — the statutory definitions, the hands-on personal care prohibition quoted as the forbidden zone, the client population, the services specified on the face since 1 July 2018 and the line between registration and HHA or nurse registry licensure; ownership, controlling interests, the administrator and the financial officer with the disclosures, the screening-before-appointment gate, the ownership-integrity, data-location and foreign-interest attestations and the Sunbiz consistency check; the geographic service area — one health service planning district, the county list, the address-of-record-inside rule and the advance amendments; the certificate, the name and the advertising rules with the registration number in every advertisement and the banned name terms; policy adoption, annual review and Exhibit A with the honesty note that Florida mandates no policy manual; the address of record, home office, zoning and the filed hours surveyors visit in; changes, amendments and the three reporting lanes with the $500 late fine and the change-of-ownership route; renewal and closure — the 60-to-120-day online-only window, the $25.38 late fee, THE VOID CLIFF and the discontinuance duties; and enforcement — unannounced inspections, the fine classes, moratorium, denial and revocation, the outstanding-fines bar and the duty of any person to report unlicensed providers.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$50.75 two-year registration, the before-contact screening gate, 5-business-day status reports, 5-year rescreens, the 90-day break rule, three change lanes with the $500 fine, the $1,000/day boundary stack, the VOID renewal cliff — exactly as the texts state them.

What is moving in Florida right now

A registration, not a licence: no training hours, no RN, no bond, no survey before you start — this manual says so plainly instead of inventing requirements. What Florida does enforce is the machinery: the Clearinghouse-first screening gate, the hands-on-care boundary with its $1,000-per-day stack, the three change lanes and the online-only renewal window that ends in a VOID registration, not a late fee. And Part D tracks what is moving — Rule 59A-35.060 took effect the day before this edition's capture, the fees drift with CPI, and every unread AHCA document is an honest placeholder, never a guess.

The timeline

  1. Decide the services and the face: companion services, homemaker services or both; every planned task checked against the statutory definitions (ss. 400.462(8), (22)) and nothing from s. 400.462(27); set the option switches in
  2. Set up: choose the entity or the sole-proprietor route (a sole proprietor may use the legal name and SSN); register any DBA on Sunbiz and make every name and address match the Division of Corporations record (Rule 59A-35.060(7)); screen the planned name against the banned terms.
  3. Fix the office: the address of record inside a served county of ONE health service planning district, zoning or home-occupation authority confirmed (Rule 59A-35.100), the hours of operation chosen as hours the office will really keep.
  4. People: identify every controlling interest (officers, directors, 5%+ owners, and the same tiers of any management company — s. 408.803(7)) with SSNs or EINs ready; name the administrator and the financial officer and have each screened and qualified BEFORE appointment (Rule 59A-35.090(2)(e);); run the Section 7 and 8 disclosure questions .
  5. Manual before filing: complete your policy manual, adopt it and print Exhibit A, so the attestations the application signs — screening, data location, ownership — are true on the Agency's own paper before they are signed.
  6. File: Form 3110-1003 (July 2024) Sections 1–12 with the Addendum and the $50.75 fee — online through the Online Licensing System (submit and pay within 30 days of creating the application) or on paper to the Long Term Care Services Unit, Mail Stop 33; record the filing date and everything sent; fees are nonrefundable.
  7. The file number and the screening: when the AHCA file number letter arrives, open the Clearinghouse account, initiate every required check CLEARINGHOUSE-FIRST, send each person to the Livescan vendor only after initiation, and track every result to "eligible" or "exemption on file" and — the application is not complete until all required screening results are in (Rule 59A-35.060(9)(b)).
  8. Answer and wait: respond to any omissions notice within 21 days (withdrawal and forfeited fees otherwise — s. 408.806(3)(b)), log every contact, and await the decision within 60 days of completeness; there is no initial inspection and no published processing time beyond these clocks, and your policy manual prints none.
  9. On approval: enter [REGISTRATION NUMBER], [EXPIRY DATE], the services on the face and the counties served, and; diarise day minus 120 and the hard minus-60 renewal deadline.
  10. Open: display the certificate conspicuously at the address on its face (s. 408.804(2);); give every client the three first-day notices on or before the first day of service and record them (s. 408.810(5)); open the eight-element client record at acceptance (Rule 59A-8.025(8);); print the registration number in every advertisement (s. 400.509(5);).
  11. Operate the machinery: the before-contact gate on every hire (s. 435.06(2)(a);); the Form 3100-0008 attestation and eligibility result in every personnel file (Rule 59A-35.090(2)(d), (3)(c);); initial status and every change reported to the Clearinghouse within 5 business days (s. 435.12(2)(c)2.;); the two-attempt employment-history verification (s. 400.509(4); Rule 59A-8.025(7);); the 5-year rescreens and the 90-day break monitor (ss. 408.809(2), 435.12(2);); the employer attestation annually or at renewal (s. 435.05(3);).
  12. Report every change in its lane: 21 days AFTER for application-information changes including administrator changes and any carried insurance or bond (s. 408.810(3); Rule 59A-35.110(1)); 21–120 days BEFORE for address-of-record moves and 60–120 days BEFORE for services and counties, with the $500 late fine and the rule that operating at a new address first is unlicensed activity (Rule 59A-35.040); change of ownership at least 60 days ahead, paper, with the seller's letter and the repayment gate (ss. 408.806(2)(b), 408.831(2)); mailing-address-only changes by email (AHCA page — paraphrase, verify); all .
  13. Renew and, one day, close: renew ONLINE ONLY in the 60-to-120-day window — more than 120 days early is returned, less than 60 days late enters the $25.38 fee zone, and after expiry the registration is VOID: stop operating, place clients with a registered provider, file a new initial application (ss. 408.806(2), (6); Rule 59A-35.060(2), (6), (11);). On discontinuance, inform AHCA not less than 30 days ahead, inform clients, surrender the certificate immediately, and forward records per each client's choice or publish the 4-consecutive-week newspaper notice (s. 408.810(4);). Re-check Part D at each annual review and before every print run.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The services decided and matched to the face: companion services, homemaker services or both; every planned task inside the statutory definitions (ss. 400.462(8), (22)) and nothing from the "personal care" definition (s. 400.462(27)); the scope statement adopted
  • The entity or sole-proprietor route chosen; any DBA registered on Sunbiz with every name and address matching the Division of Corporations record; the provider name screened against AHCA's banned terms (home health, nursing, personal care, patient care, health care, medical, assisted living, CNA, health professional); no advertising run before a registration number exists
  • The address of record fixed inside a served county of ONE health service planning district, zoning or home-occupation authority confirmed, and hours of operation chosen that the office will really keep — surveyors visit during them
  • The administrator and the financial officer named (they may be the same person) and each screened and qualified BEFORE appointment
  • Every controlling interest listed — officers, directors, 5%+ owners and the same tiers of any management company — with SSNs or EINs ready and the Section 7 and 8 disclosures prepared
  • The ownership-integrity check run: no ownership interest held by a person with a disqualifying history, and the data-location and foreign-country-of-concern attestations true before they are signed
  • The $50.75 fee ready — cheque or money order payable to "Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA)" on paper, or card/e-Check online — knowing fees are nonrefundable
  • Form 3110-1003 (July 2024) Sections 1–12 completed with the Addendum, and the filing route chosen: online (submit and pay within 30 days of creating the application) or paper to Mail Stop 33
  • The Clearinghouse plan ready: the AHCA file number awaited by letter, every check initiated CLEARINGHOUSE-FIRST before any Livescan visit, and a Livescan vendor located
  • Every companion, sitter and homemaker tracked to "eligible" or "exemption on file" BEFORE any client contact, with the signed Form 3100-0008 attestation and the eligibility result in every personnel file
  • The two-attempt employment and contract history verification completed and documented for everyone who will have in-home client contact
  • The three first-day notices printed in the statute's exact words with the toll-free numbers verified, and the s. 408.810(5)(b) notice policy adopted — the one written policy Florida requires
  • The eight-element confidential client record set up, with the acceptance standard understood: a reasonable expectation of adequate and safe service, capable workers, all visits covered
  • The compliance calendar loaded: [EXPIRY DATE], renewal preparation from day minus 120 and the hard minus-60 deadline, every worker's 5-year rescreen date, the annual employer attestation, and any carried insurance or bond
  • The three change lanes posted where the manager works: 21 days after for information changes; 21–120 days before for address moves; 60–120 days before for services and counties — with the $500 late fine and the new-location trap
  • This manual adopted and Exhibit A printed under its cover sheet as the inspection production index
  • The inspection posture set: certificate displayed conspicuously, the office staffed during the filed hours, records producible at no cost, and the duty of any person to report unlicensed providers understood — competitors watch the boundary
  • The HCS State Regulation Set requested from AHCA's Aspen survey page and aligned to it line by line when obtained [verify]
  • The Addendum 3110-1024 edition confirmed at filing and the checklist Form 3110-1003CL obtained [verify]
  • The hotline numbers for the first-day notices confirmed as AHCA currently prescribes them [verify]

What the regulator reads before you are licensed

Florida mandates almost no policy content — the statute requires exactly one written policy — and verifies you by unannounced inspections at the hours you filed, where records must be producible at no cost.
