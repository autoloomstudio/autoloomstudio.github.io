How to get a Washington in-home services agency license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Chapter 70.127 RCW and Chapter 246-335 WAC · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Washington, you need a In-Home Services Agency License (Home Care) from the Washington State Department of Health before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as an in-home services agency for the home care category — the orientation class, the ten application items, the $5,000 fee and county surcharge, the nine-month window, the announced initial survey, the twelve-month initial licence, twenty-four-month renewals filed thirty days before expiry, the renewal fee bands, the late fee and the displayed licence; scope of services — home care services, nonmedical services, the ten ADLs and seven IADLs with their defined limits, respite, the boundary with home health and clinical judgment, protected terms, exemptions and the optional services declared; ownership, officers, managing employees and ten-percent owners, fitness and the twenty-four grounds, the durable power of attorney prohibition; the administrator as a home care employee with qualifications set by Agency policy, the written alternate and the fourteen duties; the plan of operation and its management-and-supervision criteria for every approved county; policy adoption, the DOH review gate, Exhibit A, the own-words rule and accessibility of policies; the in-state office, branch offices, approved counties and service-area expansion; change of ownership, the five thirty-day notices, ceasing operation and exemption requests; advertising, protected terms and accuracy of public information; surveys, statements of deficiencies, plans of correction, conditions, civil fines, the limited stop service, hearings and unlicensed operation; client records and retention; insurance, business licences, fiscal affairs and the fee calendar.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$5,000 fee, 12-month initial licence then 24-month renewals, 9-month window, 10 application items, $1M/$2M insurance, 14 delivery policies, 14 personnel policies, 10-item personnel file, 19-item bill of rights, 7-day start of care, 48-hour discharge notice, 6-month client contact, 12-month plan-of-care review, 75 training hours in 120 days with 5 before care, certification applied for in 14 days, checks renewed every 2 years — exactly as the texts state them.

What is moving in Washington right now

Washington licenses non-medical home care through the Department of Health, inside the same statute as home health and hospice. After the application is accepted, the Office of Investigation and Inspection requests the policy manual and a registered nurse surveyor reviews it at the announced initial survey; applicants must pass for the licence to issue. DOH's own guidance says template and AI-generated manuals must be thoroughly reviewed by the applicant so they reflect real workflows — so this manual marks every customisation point, and Part D lists every point to verify when DOH, the Legislature or the rules move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: form the entity, obtain the UBI number and the FEIN, and obtain a business licence for each office from the state and any county or city that requires one; choose the in-state main office where records will be kept, secured and accessible, any branch office, the county or counties of the service area and the days and hours of operation; decide every option switch and write them into; buy the commercial general liability policy at the rule's minimum — $1,000,000 per occurrence, or $2,000,000 as a combined single limit — and register for workers' compensation and unemployment insurance "[verify Washington L&I and ESD requirements]". Do not advertise, hold out or use "home care" in a name before the licence issues.
  2. Orientation class: register for the free online DOH orientation class, attend the four-hour session, and file the certificate — it is valid for one year and the application is not processed without it (WAC 246-335-320(1);).
  3. Key posts: name the administrator — a home care employee who meets the qualification the Agency's policy sets — and the supervisor of direct care services — employed, not contracted — and have each designate a written alternate; one person may hold both posts, but each post needs its own alternate. Obtain each person's Washington State Patrol check and DOH Disclosure Statement 505-055 within three months before the application date.
  4. Manual: adopt your policy manual, customise every policy — names, counties, hours, services, switches, the bracketed numbers and the CUSTOMISE line — print Exhibit A with its cover sheet, sign the plan of operation and complete the emergency plan, the bloodborne pathogens plan and the infection-control review. Check that, to 3.08 and the services list you file match word for word.
  5. File: complete the application 505-051 (type "New"), the FTE worksheet 505-137 and the organizational chart or narrative; write the management-and-supervision description for each county and the services list; assemble the insurance certificate, the two WSP checks, the two disclosure statements, the business licences and the orientation certificate; enclose the $5,000 fee plus any county surcharge by check or money order payable to the Department of Health; mail the package to P.O. Box 1099, Olympia, WA 98507-1099, and record the date sent — the nine-month clock and the refund rules run from the submission date (WAC 246-335-320(4); 990(9)–(10)).
  6. DOH review: answer any letter about outstanding documentation or fees by return and log it (DOH 505-053); do not send the manual until OII asks for it (DOH 505-109).
  7. OII request and initial announced survey: when the survey office requests the manual, send Exhibit A and the policies it maps, customised, together with the forms; prepare two complete personnel files, the client packet and the staff for interview; the surveyors review the policies, sample files, staff knowledge and care delivery (WAC 246-335-320(3); DOH Survey Program page — paraphrase).
  8. Statement of deficiencies: if one issues, enter the ten-working-day due date the day it arrives, write the plan of correction on the DOH form with the four elements for each deficiency, sign and return it by the date, complete every correction within sixty days unless DOH allows longer, and keep the file — a follow-up compliance survey costs $5,000 (WAC 246-335-345; 990(6)(a);).
  9. Licence issued: an initial licence for twelve months for the home care category and the approved county or counties — record the licence number, issue date and expiry; display the licence in the main office where the public can see it and in any branch office (WAC 246-335-335(3);); put the licence number and category on the service agreement, the bill of rights and all advertising, now that the protected terms may be used.
  10. Workforce: enrol with the DSHS Background Check Central Unit as an authorised entity "[process — verify]"; for every worker, before any direct client contact — the application, references and the DOH credential search; the applicant notice, disclosure, WSP check with the result in hand and the DOH disclosure statement; the DSHS state and FBI fingerprint check with the state result in hand; the signed job description; for every long-term care worker the two orientation hours and three safety hours before any care and the certification application within fourteen calendar days of hire; the Agency's in-person orientation, TB training, confidentiality agreement and written test; the TB risk assessment on the DOH form; skills verification before independent care; and the roster entry with the ADL-eligibility column. Track the 120-day training clock and the earlier of the 200-day and the 365-day certification clocks for each worker.
  11. First clients: the referral logged with the seven-day start date and any documented exception; the scope screen against the ADL and IADL limits and the clinical-judgment line; the written bill of rights, consent, payment source and the service agreement at admission; the on-site visit and the plan of care with the client's written approval before the first service; the per-client back-up plan; the medication list and permitted acts where MEDICATION ASSISTANCE = YES; the signed and dated contact note from the first visit; the Agency's in-home supervisory visit within [30] days.
  12. Continuing clocks: the supervisor's documented client contact by phone or visit every six months; the on-site plan-of-care review every twelve months and on any significant change; annual performance evaluations with on-site observation and annual contractor evaluations; annual TB risk assessments on the DOH form; the supervisor's and alternate's ten training hours a year; the annual infection-control review and the annual bloodborne-pathogen re-check; the two-year WSP check and disclosure statement renewal for everyone; the monthly and annual exemption re-tests for 20-hour and respite-only workers; the fourteen-day DOH noncompliance report and the immediate APS report whenever they arise; the quarterly QI meeting and the annual satisfaction survey; the annual policy review; re-check the rule.
  13. Unannounced survey: at least once each licensure period, at any time, with in-home visits with client consent; produce the displayed licence, the roster, the personnel files, the client records, the policies with Exhibit A, the insurance certificate, the plan of operation and any approved exemption; answer any statement of deficiencies as in step 8.
  14. Renewal: at least thirty days before the expiry date file the renewal application, continuing insurance evidence, the FTE worksheet, the administrator's and supervisor's current WSP checks and disclosure statements (renewed within two years of the previous ones), the current organizational structure, anything else DOH asks for, and the fee for your FTE band — $2,400, $2,800, $3,000, $3,500 or $3,700; the late fee of $50 a day runs from the renewal date, thirty days before expiry, to a cap of $1,000 (WAC 246-335-325; 990(4), (8);). Each renewal licence runs twenty-four months.
  15. Changes: written notice to DOH within thirty days whenever the administrator or the supervisor of direct care services changes, an office opens or closes, home care services cease or the service area shrinks; an amended application with prior DOH approval and the county surcharge before serving a new county; the change-of-ownership form and the new owner's application at least thirty days before any transfer, dissolution, merger, consolidation or UBI/FEIN change, with $500 per category — approved exemptions do not transfer; on closure, inform each client of alternative services, transfer each client with a summary of care, and preserve or dispose of the records under the retention rules.
  16. Public programmes: DSHS contracts are not available to a newly licensed home care agency (DOH 505-109); if the Agency later contracts with DSHS or an Area Agency on Aging, joins the WA Cares Fund network or serves a managed care plan, those programmes set their own requirements (not reproduced), the contracted fee table and the substantial-equivalency route may then apply, and the contract-specific time frames in WAC 246-335-420(1)(d), 420(3)(d) and 450(7)(d) must be written into the policies they touch — keep those policies in a separate binder.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity formed; UBI number and FEIN obtained; a current business licence held for each office location from the state and any county or city that requires one
  • In-state main office chosen where records will be kept, secured and accessible; any branch office named; the county or counties of the service area decided, with the urban or rural surcharge worked out for any county beyond the first; days and hours of operation stated
  • Commercial general liability insurance in force at the rule's minimum ($1,000,000 per occurrence, or $2,000,000 as a combined single limit), with the certificate in hand; workers' compensation and unemployment insurance registrations confirmed
  • DOH orientation class attended and the certificate of completion in hand, dated within the last twelve months
  • Administrator named (a home care employee meeting the Agency's own qualification) with a written alternate; supervisor of direct care services employed (not contracted) with a written similarly qualified alternate; each post's alternate a different person from the other post's holder where one person holds both
  • Washington State Patrol criminal history background check and DOH Disclosure Statement 505-055 obtained for the administrator and the supervisor, each dated within three months before the application date, the disclosure statements signed by the person and a witness
  • Organizational chart or narrative written, naming officers, administrator and alternate, supervisor and alternate and other key positions, with the identity of the applicant, partners, managing employees and owners of ten percent or more
  • Management-and-supervision description written for each county to the four criteria — intake and plan of care; supervision and contractor monitoring; evaluations; quality improvement — and the list of services provided directly and under contract matching the services statement and the service policies word for word
  • Every option switch decided and written into the plan of operation and services statement; the three optional services each declared NO or YES; the "home care category only" sentence included
  • Manual adopted and customised — agency name, counties, hours, services, switches, bracketed numbers and every CUSTOMISE line completed — with Exhibit A printed under its cover sheet, ready for the survey office's request but not sent with the application
  • Application 505-051 (type "New") and FTE worksheet 505-137 completed and signed; the fee of $5,000 plus any county surcharge ready as a check or money order payable to the Department of Health; the mailing address P.O. Box 1099, Olympia, WA 98507-1099 on the envelope; the submission date to be recorded for the nine-month clock
  • Two complete personnel files assembled in the ten-item order, with the administrator's and supervisor's designations, alternates, duty acknowledgments and the supervisor's ten-hour training log opened
  • Hiring system ready before the first worker: application with references and credential search; applicant notice, disclosure and WSP check with the result before direct contact; DOH disclosure statement; DSHS fingerprint check through the Background Check Central Unit with the state result before direct contact; signed job description; roster with the ADL-eligibility column
  • Training and certification tracking ready: two orientation hours and three safety hours before any care; the DSHS-approved seventy-five-hour curriculum identified; the certification application within fourteen calendar days of hire; the 120-day training clock and the earlier of the 200-day and 365-day certification clocks on the tracker; exemption bases and re-test dates recorded
  • Agency orientation, TB training, confidentiality agreement, written test and skills verification checklist ready for every worker before independent care; the DOH tuberculosis risk assessment form obtained and the Hepatitis B offer letter ready
  • Client packet ready: written bill of rights with the nineteen rights, the supervisor's name and contact method, the DOH complaint number (verified) and the DSHS End Harm number; service agreement with charges and the itemised statement on request; consent and representative record; admission record; plan of care with the six contents and the client's written approval; back-up plan; contact note; discharge notice
  • Reporting routes written into the policies and taught at orientation: suspected abandonment, abuse, financial exploitation or neglect of a vulnerable adult reported immediately by the observing worker to DSHS Adult Protective Services and the proper law enforcement agency, with the numbers verified before printing; noncompliance with the chapter reported to DOH on its form within fourteen calendar days; 9-1-1 in an emergency
  • Emergency preparedness plan, bloodborne pathogens exposure control plan and infection-control checklist complete, with the annual review of health-authority recommendations dated and the client priority list format ready
  • Licence and compliance calendar set: twelve-month initial expiry; renewal date thirty days before expiry; two-year check renewals; annual TB assessments, evaluations, contractor evaluations, supervisor hours and infection-control review; six-month client contacts; twelve-month plan-of-care reviews; insurance and business-licence expiries
  • Marketing and website use "home care" and "in-home services" only after the licence issues, state the licence number and category, make no home health, nursing, therapy or hospice claim, and reference no programme or certification the Agency does not hold
