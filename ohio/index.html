How to get an Ohio nonmedical home health services license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to R.C. Chapter 3740 and OAC Chapter 3701-60 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Ohio, you need a Nonmedical Home Health Services License from the Ohio Department of Health — Bureau of Regulatory Operations before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as a home health agency — the nonmedical home health services license, the application, the $250 fee, issuance without a site visit, the three-year term and renewal at least ninety days before expiry; scope of services and the boundary with skilled home health services, and who is a "home health agency" and a "nonagency provider"; ownership, officers, the statutory agent, the primary owner's fingerprint impressions and the application disclosures; the chief administrator, organisational structure and delegation; the $20,000 surety bond and bond-exempt status; policy adoption, annual review and the two application exhibits; the operating location, branches, days and hours, counties served and the Ohio administrative office; the ten-day change notice and change of ownership; advertising and holding out; ODH requests for information, the compliance attestation, complaint investigations, enforcement, Chapter 119 hearings and informal dispute resolution; client records and retention; books and records, insurance, workers' compensation, closure and continuity of care.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$250 fee, $20,000 bond, 3-year licence, renewal 90 days before expiry, 10-day change notice, 14 days to answer an RFI, 60 days to process, 6 databases, BCI check requested within 5 business days, 60-day conditional limit, 5-year re-checks, 5 roster fields, 3 medication acts, no site visit — exactly as the texts state them.

What is moving in Ohio right now

The nonmedical licence has been mandatory since 2022 and OAC 3701-60 took effect January 27, 2023, with a five-year review due in 2028. The chapter governs licensing and background checks only, so this manual labels every operational policy as the Agency's own and says where a nearby Ohio benchmark exists. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when ODH, the rules or the General Assembly move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: form the entity and file with the Ohio Secretary of State; name the statutory agent and keep the Secretary of State record exact; obtain the IRS Employer Identification Number; [for an out-of-state agency, establish the Ohio administrative office first — OAC 3701-60-03(D)]; choose the operating location and any branches, the days and hours of operation and the counties; appoint the chief administrator; arrange Ohio BWC workers' compensation [verify] and [general and professional liability — Agency standard; no Ohio text requires insurance beyond the bond]; decide every option switch and write them into; adopt your policy manual and complete; and with the direct care designation signed. Do not provide or advertise nonmedical home health services before the licence issues (R.C. 3740.02(A)(2); § 3740.99).
  2. Exhibits: print Exhibit A with the official text of OAC 3701-60-08 and 3701-60-09 attached, and Exhibit B, each under its cover sheet; check that, the policies and the counties match word for word.
  3. Fingerprints and bond: the primary owner's fingerprint impression card at an Ohio Attorney General Webcheck location or local law enforcement; the $20,000 surety bond on the ODH form from a surety licensed in Ohio, or the pre-September 30, 2021 evidence; enter the bond's effective and expiry dates and the 90-day cancellation watch .
  4. File: form HEA 0622 "Initial" with the nine checklist items and the $250 fee, all together, on the ODH Gateway ("CertLicensure") or by mail to Revenue Processing #3506; save the two automatic e-mails and the payment receipt.
  5. Processing: answer any request for information within fourteen days of its mailing date (OAC 3701-60-03(G);); allow 60 days; there is no site visit; a denial carries a Chapter 119 hearing and informal dispute resolution (OAC 3701-60-04(B)–(C)).
  6. Licence issued: record the licence number and State ID, the issue and expiry dates, the renewal filing date (expiry minus 90 days) and the bond expiry; put the licence category and number on the service agreement, the rights notice and advertising; check the entry on the Health Care Provider Services lookup.
  7. Staff: for every direct care position, the fingerprinting and fee notice, the six-database review, the BCI check with FBI information where required, conditional employment only on a clear database review, a completed fingerprint sheet before the first day and the request made within five business days, the offence determination, the sealed file and the roster entry — then orientation, competency verification and the written test before any unsupervised assignment.
  8. First clients: intake and scope screening; the service agreement with its disclosures; the in-home assessment and service plan; the rights notice; the medication assistance record where the three acts are provided; daily documentation from day one; the first supervisory visit within [14] days and thereafter at least every [60] days.
  9. Continuing: written notice to the director within ten days of any change to the application information — name, address, telephone, owners, officers, statutory agent, services, hours, counties, the criminal records check policy; a change of ownership is a new application; the roster kept current and the attestation given on request; re-checks no later than thirty days after each employee's fifth anniversary of hire and at least every five years thereafter, with the database review repeated first; the quarterly self-audit; the annual policy review, in-service programme, evaluations and emergency plan review; re-check the rule.
  10. Every three years: file the renewal — form HEA 0622 "Renewal" with the State ID, only the changed information, and the $250 renewal fee — at least ninety days before the expiry date, with the bond continued by certificate for the new term; "Fails to timely renew it's license" is a ground for denial, suspension or revocation (OAC 3701-60-04(A)(2)), and no late fee, grace period or reinstatement rule appears in any text — treat the 90-day point as the hard internal deadline. The triennial renewal period announced by ODH "begins July 31, 2025" [confirm the Agency's own expiry date on the licence — Part D].
  11. Public programmes: after licensure the Agency may seek Ohio Department of Aging certification under R.C. 173.391 or Medicaid provider enrolment (Ohio Department of Medicaid, 1-800-686-1516); those programmes set their own standards (OAC 173-39; OAC 5160-45, 5160-46 — not reproduced) — keep their policies in a separate binder, and remember that ODA certification changes the application items and that a Medicaid or waiver agency adds the seventh disclosure class for BCI reports (rule 3701-60-10(A)(7)).

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity formed and every document filed and recorded with the Ohio Secretary of State copied; statutory agent named exactly as the Secretary of State shows it; IRS Employer Identification Number obtained
  • ONE business type chosen to match the Secretary of State record; every owner of 5% or more, every officer, member, trustee or director and the statutory agent listed with addresses and telephone numbers
  • The five-year affiliation question and the conviction, judgment and adjudication question answered for every owner and the administrator, with written explanations ready where the answer is yes
  • Operating location and any branches fixed, with the full address including suite number, county, telephone and e-mail; days and hours of operation stated Monday to Sunday; counties served listed
  • Chief administrator appointed in writing with a named designee; direct care positions designated by job title and the designation signed
  • Services list written in the rule's own words with the counties served, matching the service policies word for word; no skilled service, catheter insertion or medication act beyond the three permitted acts anywhere in it
  • Exhibit A printed: the written criminal records check policy under its cover sheet, with the official text of OAC 3701-60-08 and 3701-60-09 attached from codes.ohio.gov
  • Exhibit B printed: the services list with the home health aide, personal care and medication-assistance policies under its cover sheet
  • Primary owner's fingerprint impression card taken, inked or electronic, at an Ohio Attorney General Webcheck location or by local law enforcement
  • $20,000 surety bond on the ODH form issued by a surety licensed in Ohio, completed and notarised, with the 90-day cancellation watch entered — or the evidence of operation on or immediately prior to September 30, 2021
  • Form HEA 0622 completed as "Initial", all 25 questions answered, signed by a named owner or with the notarised authorised-representative affidavit; the $250 fee ready to pay with the application, not separately
  • Filing route chosen — the ODH Gateway ("CertLicensure") with card or e-check, or mail to Revenue Processing #3506 with a check or money order to "Treasurer, State of Ohio"; the two automatic e-mails and the payment receipt saved
  • Request-for-information log opened with the 14-day rule from the mailing date written on it; the 60-day processing expectation on the calendar
  • Hiring system ready before the first direct care worker: fingerprinting and fee notice, six-database review record, BCI and FBI forms and fingerprint sheets, conditional employment attestation, offence worksheet, sealed file and the five-field roster
  • Orientation, competency checklist covering every listed service and the three medication acts, written test and TB screening ready for every worker before an unsupervised assignment
  • Client packet ready: service agreement stating the licence category and number, no skilled licence, not Medicare-certified, the ODH complaint line 1-800-342-0553 and the county APS route; rights notice; service plan; medication record; visit note
  • County department of job and family services APS contacts for every county served entered, with 9-1-1 for emergencies; every employee oriented to the duty to report immediately
  • Licence calendar set: three-year expiry, renewal filing date at expiry minus 90 days, bond expiry and continuation, five-year re-check dates, ten-day change notice log, quarterly self-audit
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a provider of nonmedical home health services under its ODH licence, never imply skilled services or Medicare certification, and appear only after the licence issues
