How to get a Minnesota basic home care provider licence in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Minn. Stat. §§ 144A.43–144A.4799 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Minnesota, you need a Basic Home Care Provider Licence from the Minnesota Department of Health — Health Regulation Division before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as a basic home care provider — the licence requirement in the statute's own gate sentence, the two licence levels, the § 144A.471 subd 6 basic service set quoted whole, the exemptions and exclusions as summarised, and the key definitions; the basic/comprehensive boundary with the § 144A.4792 and § 144A.4793 prohibition sentences verbatim, the no-delegated-tasks rule, standby-not-hands-on, the complicating-eating-problems line, and the ch. 144G, § 144A.482 and no-Medicare boundaries; application and the temporary licence with the § 144A.472 subd 1 contents, the $2,100 fee, pre-licence background studies, workers' compensation evidence, the 14-day acknowledgment and the 90-day decision; serving clients and the conversion survey with the five-day notice and its $1,000 fine, the 90-day survey window, substantial compliance, extension with conditions and the 15-calendar-day reconsideration; renewal and fees with the verbatim revenue-tiered table, the highest-fee election, the double-fee penalty and the 10% clean-survey discount; change of ownership with the new-application rule, inherited fines and corrections, and the 2026 employee-continuity amendment with its 90-day differing-policies clock; notification of changes on the ten-working-day rule and the MDH forms tracker; surveys, correction orders and the verbatim five-level fine schedule with the two reconsideration lanes never merged; enforcement, appeals and closure with the hearing clocks and the three-day client-list and notification duties; and display, advertising and the provider restrictions.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$2,100 application fee, the 14-day acknowledgment and the 90-day decision, the five-day serving-clients notice with its $1,000 fine, the 90-day no-notice survey window, the 14-day service plan and 30-day initial review, the 10-day termination notice, fines of $500 to $5,000 by level, and the 10% clean-survey renewal discount — exactly as the statute states them.

What is moving in Minnesota right now

Minnesota licenses you provisionally, then comes to look: within five days of your first client you must notify MDH (a $1,000 fine if you miss it), the survey arrives without notice within 90 days of that notice, and the full licence issues only on substantial compliance. The timeline

  1. Choose the lane: new provider, or change of ownership — a new application on the same fee, filed before the acquisition closes, with the 2026 employee-continuity rules behind it
  2. Form the entity and gather the ownership attachments — legal name exactly as filed with the Minnesota Secretary of State, federal and state tax IDs, and the attachments MDH's form requires by entity type
  3. Enrol in NETStudy 2.0 and clear the pre-licence background studies — every owner and managerial official must complete and pass a study before the temporary licence issues
  4. Secure workers' compensation evidence — a certificate of coverage, not an application — or make the no-employees election and notify MDH on first hiring
  5. Assemble and file the application to the § 144A.472 subd 1 contents with the $2,100 fee — cheques payable to the Minnesota Department of Health, all fees nonrefundable, and an application without the fee is incomplete
  6. The 14-day acknowledgment: MDH says in writing whether the application is complete — answer every deficiency request promptly, because the decision clock runs only from a complete application
  7. The decision: the statute's 90-day clock from a complete application, beside MDH's own 60-day commitment — the only two processing figures any captured text publishes
  8. The temporary year opens — the licence is effective for up to one year and every home care duty is live from day one
  9. Within five days of serving your first client, notify MDH with proof of sending — the fine for missing the notice is $1,000, and if no services are provided during the temporary period the licence expires and the fees were nonrefundable
  10. The no-notice conversion survey arrives within 90 days of the notice — prepare from the statute's own ten-policy index and the survey production index
  11. Conversion on substantial compliance — or termination, or extension with conditions for up to 90 days, with the temporary licensee's 15-calendar-day reconsideration lane
  12. Licence in hand, load the clocks: annual renewal at least 30 days before expiry on the revenue-tiered fee table, the 10-working-day notification of changes, and the 10% renewal discount a violation-free survey earns

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The application lane chosen — new provider or change of ownership — knowing a Minnesota home care licence can never be bought with the business
  • The services, staffing, electronic records, client finances and application lane option switches set, and every bracketed field filled
  • The entity formed, with the legal name exactly matching the Minnesota Secretary of State filing and the IRS 147-C tax letter in hand
  • The ownership attachments obtained per MDH's current application form, by entity type
  • NETStudy 2.0 enrolment done and every owner's and managerial official's background study completed and passed before filing
  • Workers' compensation evidence in hand — a certificate of coverage, not an application — or the no-employees election understood, with coverage obtained and MDH notified on first hiring
  • The statute's ten required policies of § 144A.472 subd 1(14) adopted and dated, each mapped to the policy that answers it
  • The $2,100 application fee drawn correctly — cheque payable to the Minnesota Department of Health, no starter or counter cheques, all fees nonrefundable
  • The application filed through the FPLS portal or by mail with every § 144A.472 subd 1 content item present
  • The 14-day acknowledgment received and every deficiency request answered promptly — the decision clock runs only from a complete application
  • The temporary-year clocks loaded on day one: the licence effective for up to one year, every home care duty live from the start
  • The five-day serving-clients notice ready to send with proof of sending — the fine for missing it is $1,000
  • Staff oriented before providing services, training and competency evaluations complete, TB screenings on file per current CDC standards
  • The bill-of-rights notice and statement of services given before first service, the service plan finalised within 14 days, the initial review within 30 days at the client's residence
  • The survey production posture standing, with the client list producible promptly — Minnesota surveys arrive without advance notice
  • Any correction order answered on time, with the temporary licensee's 15-calendar-day reconsideration clock in view
  • After conversion: the original licence displayed at the principal business office and renewal armed at least 30 days before expiry on the revenue-tiered fee table
  • Monitoring reviews cycling at least every 90 days, the eight-hour annual training cycling, quality-management reviews documented and kept two years
  • The 10-working-day notification-of-changes lane live, and the watch items diarised: successor guidance on the 2026 change-of-ownership amendment, the current ESST and MN Paid Leave figures
