How to get a Georgia private home care provider license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 111-8-65 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Georgia, you need a Private Home Care Provider (PHCP) License from the Georgia Department of Community Health — Healthcare Facility Regulation Division before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure for two service categories, fees, renewal and the notices after it; scope of services and types of clients; the governing body and the statement of responsibility; the administrator's three qualifications; the seven mandated policies and the Affidavit of Compliance; inspections, consent to entry and the ten-day plan of correction; enforcement, fines, public reprimands, waivers and variances; the quality improvement programme; client records and five-year retention; charges, cancellation and client funds; insurance, bonding, vehicle cover, business registration and contracted services; staffing sufficiency and shortage referral.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$300 + $250 per category, 15 days' notice of a move, 10 days for a plan of correction, agreement by the 2nd visit or 7 days, plan in 7 working days, visits every 92 / 122 days, 20 + 20 training hours, 8 annual hours, 30-day grace period, 5-year records — exactly as the rule states them.

What is moving in Georgia right now

This manual applies Rules .03, .05 and .09 as adopted in 2026 — owner and employee fingerprint checks through Chapter 111-8-12, a health screening programme with CDC-based baseline TB screening — and still answers the packet's older wording (the administrator's no-misconduct attestation, TB testing results) so that whichever text a reviewer applies, you comply. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when the rule, the packet or the fees move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: register the entity with the Secretary of State and obtain the IRS Tax ID; obtain the city or county business licence; secure the licensed office (one licence per location); form the governing body and pass the resolutions; register a facility account in GCHEXS; run registry checks and GCHEXS applications for every owner, the administrator and any manager — allow 24–48 hours for GBI/FBI results plus 10–15 business days for the Department's determination; appoint the administrator only when the determination is in hand; decide Option A or B for medically frail clients and who will direct the 40-hour programme; adopt your policy manual and complete and; obtain insurance and, if needed, bonding; recruit, check, screen, orient, train and evaluate at least one PCA or companion so that two complete personnel files exist; build the client packet. Do not sign a client or provide a service before the licence issues (§ 31-7-301), and — as the Agency's own standard — do not advertise before it either.
  2. Apply in GAMMIS with the thirteen items and the $300 + $500 fees; keep the LTN; answer the application unit's e-mails promptly; expect about sixty days from confirmation of receipt.
  3. Licence issued (or a provisional licence for a new provider to demonstrate compliance — r. .05(1)(c)); display it; enter the renewal and fee dates; verify your listing on the Department's licence search.
  4. First clients: referral and initial contact dates recorded; medically frail screening; service agreement and rights notice by the second visit on a different day or seven calendar days; service plan within seven working days; first supervisory visit within 92 days (personal care) or 122 days (companion); daily documentation from day one.
  5. After licensure: pay each category's $250 annual fee on time (late fee $150 at 60 days); renew with the description of services and geographic area; give 15 days' notice of a change of location and return the licence on any change of name, location, ownership or governing body; a new owner applies for a Change of Ownership (the Agency must have active clients); keep client records five years; run the QI programme and the annual self-audit; re-check the rule at each annual review.
  6. Public programmes: after licensure you may seek enrolment with Medicaid waiver programmes (through GAMMIS), the Department of Human Services' Division of Aging Services or Veterans Affairs; those programmes set their own requirements — keep their policies in a separate binder.

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity registered with the Secretary of State; IRS Tax ID; city/county business licence; licensed office with secure record storage and a telephone answered during stated hours and on call
  • Governing body identified; resolutions passed; statement of responsibility and Personal Identification Affidavit ready; organisational chart
  • GCHEXS facility account; satisfactory determination letters dated within 12 months for every owner, the administrator and every manager; registry and licensing-board checks dated before each GCHEXS application
  • Administrator appointed after the determination, with job description, attestations and orientation evidence; acting administrator designated
  • The seven mandated policies adopted and completed and signed; your policy manual adopted; started
  • Description of services and types of clients with the geographic area and the certified-nurse-aide statement; Option A or B chosen in and
  • Training director for the 40-hour programme named or CNA/examination-only hiring decided
  • At least two complete personnel files : registry check before GCHEXS; determination or grace-period log; baseline TB before the first day; qualification route; competency checklist before assignment; orientation with the four subjects
  • Supervisors appointed with the qualification determination recorded; on-call rota staffed — and, for Option B, the nurse supervisor reachable
  • Insurance certificates (general and professional liability; workers' compensation; hired and non-owned motor with medical payments); bonding if unlimited access to client funds is ever authorised; driver approvals
  • Client packet: service agreement with the ten items and Schedule A; rights notice with the eleven items, the supervisor, the complaints designee and the Department's address and Complaint Intake number completed; service plan form; emergency sheet; home folder set
  • Client record system; tracker for the agreement, rights notice, plan and visit deadlines
  • Complaint, change-in-condition, incident, APS and discharge forms ready; the administrator and supervisors know the APS number (1-866-552-4464) and the Complaint Intake number ((800) 878-6442)
  • QI log opened; annual self-audit scheduled; calendar started with licence, fee, insurance, training, screening and visit dates
  • Document map completed; everything producible without unreasonable delay
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a licensed private home care provider offering personal care and companion or sitter services, and appear only after the licence issues
