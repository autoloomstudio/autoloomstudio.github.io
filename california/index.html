How to get a California home care organization license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Health & Safety Code Division 2, Chapter 13 and CDSS HCS forms/PINs · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in California, you need a Home Care Organization (HCO) License from the California Department of Social Services — Home Care Services Branch before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as a Home Care Organization — the HCS 281 packet with the Section A forms and Section B documents, the one-packet rule and the $5,603 fee, the receipt notice and HCO number, the introductory letter and analyst, the $50 virtual orientation, the Guardian background examinations, the Notification of Incomplete Application, the compliance-history review and the final determination, the two-year term with the 60-day renewal notice, and the annual-fee regime from 1 January 2029; scope of services under § 1796.12(n) — the service list, the self-administered-medication boundary, the fourteen non-HCO entities and the employment-agency distinction; ownership, the licensee background examination of the owner, every 10%+ owner and the CEO, the HCS 215 disclosures, the board statements and the prior-licensure bars; governance and the HCS 308 designee; the office, name protection, advertising and the out-of-state rules; policy adoption, annual review and Exhibit A — with the honesty note that CDSS mandates no policy manual; the conspicuous posting of the licence and business hours; changes, the $100 and $200 change fees, ceasing operation and inactive licence status after an emergency; complaints, random unannounced inspections, the records-refusal revocation rule, the notice of violation, the plan of correction and the $900-per-violation-per-day penalties; records and the administrative file; the $1,000,000/$3,000,000 insurance, workers' compensation and the $10,000 employee dishonesty bond; and the dual-regime fee calendar.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$5,603 two-year licence fee now AND the $2,802 annual fee from 1 Jan 2029 — both carried; $50 orientation, $35 aide registration, 5h entry + 5h annual training, TB 90-before/7-after, $1M/$3M + $10,000 bond, IMMEDIATE departure notice, $900/violation/day — exactly as the texts state them.

What is moving in California right now

No regulations are in force: the emergency rules were repealed in 2021 and CDSS's written-directive authority expired in 2018, so what governs is the statute, the HCS forms and the Provider Information Notices — and this manual quotes them verbatim. AB 152 replaces the two-year $5,603 renewal with a $2,802 annual anniversary fee from 1 January 2029; SB 412 adds the dementia annual-training topic from 1 January 2027. Both are carried in place, version-tagged, never merged — and Part D tells you exactly what to verify when the implementing PIN and the new fees page land.

The timeline

  1. Set up: form the entity and obtain the B1 documents — the Partnership Agreement or sealed Articles plus bylaws or Operating Agreement; fix the office (a California office holding all pertinent records if the principal place of business is out of state, with Secretary of State registration for foreign entities BEFORE arranging services — § 1796.36); choose the counties/areas served and decide every option switch in Do not arrange, advertise or use the protected terms — "home care organization", "home care", "in-home care" — or make any reference to employee bonding before the licence issues (§§ 1796.34–1796.35).
  2. People first: identify the owner (or every 10%+ owner and the CEO) for the licensee background examination (§ 1796.33), the HCS 308 designee, and every board member for the HCS 9165 statements kept at the organisation; prepare the HCS 215 disclosures and screen them against the §§ 1796.40–1796.41 bars .
  3. Money and instruments: obtain the employee dishonesty bond on HCS 402 ($10,000 minimum including third-party coverage, the State of California named as beneficiary), the $1,000,000/$3,000,000 general and professional liability certificate, and the workers' compensation policy covering affiliated home care aides (§§ 1796.37(a)(2)–(4);).
  4. Manual before filing: complete your policy manual and generate the Section B documents from it — B2 from, B3 from /, B4 from / (dementia content if applying on or after 1 January 2027 — PIN 26-02-HCS), B5 from, B6 from — and adopt the set with Exhibit A printed, so the filed papers and the operated practice never diverge.
  5. File: complete A1–A7 with original signatures in booklet order and mail ONE packet — all Section A forms, all Section B documents and the $5,603 cheque or money order payable to "California Department of Social Services" — to the Home Care Services Branch, 744 P Street, M.S. 9-14-90, Sacramento, CA 95814; keep a photocopy; record the mailing date . An incomplete packet is returned whole.
  6. Receipt and analyst: file the receipt notice, put the HCO number on all future communication, and note the assigned analyst and pending licence number from the introductory letter.
  7. Orientation: return the orientation request form with the $50.00 non-refundable fee, attend the scheduled virtual session (it is not available on demand), complete the survey and file the certificate (CDSS application-steps PDF, step 5; § 1796.37(a)(7)).
  8. Background examinations: create the Guardian applications before fingerprinting, complete LiveScan for every applicant and every individual with a 10% or greater ownership stake, and track each to "cleared" or "exemption on file"; use the transfer routes (LIC 9182/LIC 9188) where a current clearance or exemption exists (§§ 1796.23(d), 1796.24(b)(2);).
  9. Answer and wait: respond to every Notification of Incomplete Application by the noted due date and log all analyst contact . No official processing time exists — the statute sets no completeness clock, the pages state no figure, and your policy manual prints none; plan the business around the licence issuing when it issues, and do not arrange services before it does.
  10. Final determination: on grant, diarise the licence date — it drives the two-year renewal (and, from 2029, the annual anniversary fee), every aide registration anniversary and the training years — all .
  11. Open: post the licence, business hours and required information conspicuously (§ 1796.42(a);); clear every affiliated home care aide on the registry BEFORE direct client contact (§§ 1796.14(b), 1796.43(a);); deliver the five entry-level hours BEFORE presence with a client (§ 1796.44(b);); run the TB examination within the 90-days-before/7-days-after window with the certificate on file; and start the service agreements, plans, notes and the quality calendar.
  12. Operate: the immediate departure notice to CDSS whenever an aide leaves (§ 1796.43(a)(3);); the aides' two-year $35 renewals and the forfeiture watch (§§ 1796.31, 1796.26(b)(1)(D);); five annual training hours per aide per training year with the dementia topic from 1 January 2027 (§ 1796.44(c);); two-yearly TB re-examinations; the abuse-report-copy file maintained and available (§ 1796.42(e);); and the monthly posting and registry sweeps.
  13. Renew: to 31 December 2028, the $5,603 two-year renewal fee postmarked on or before expiry, on CDSS's 60-day notice — an unrenewed licence expires two years after issuance (§ 1796.37(b)–(c), current version); extract and follow the renewal-process PINs 21-06-HCS and 22-03-HCS before the first renewal [not read at edition date — verify]. From 1 January 2029: the $2,802 annual fee on or before the licence anniversary ($1,401 late fee; licences issued or renewed 1 January 2028 to 1 January 2029 pay no 2029 annual fee) (§§ 1796.37(b), 1796.49(a)(1) (operative 1 Jan 2029);).
  14. Every year and before every print run: re-check the leginfo pages for amendments to §§ 1796.10–1796.70, the CDSS fees page (no 2029 figures appear on it yet), the PIN listing for new HCS-series PINs — above all the AB 152 implementing PIN — and CDSS's "Regulations in Process" page for any regulations package or interim licensing standards; then re-check every item in Part D and log changes .

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity formed with the B1 documents in hand — Partnership Agreement or sealed/stamped Articles, bylaws or Operating Agreement, foreign-entity certificates and the affiliate diagram if applicable — and no protected term ("home care organization", "home care", "in-home care") used in any name or material before licensure
  • The California office fixed, with all pertinent records held there and the out-of-state and Secretary of State rules of § 1796.36 met where the principal place of business is elsewhere
  • The owner, every 10%-or-greater owner and the CEO identified for the background examination, each holding a qualifying identification card; the HCS 215 disclosures drafted and screened against the two-year prior-revocation and one-year prior-denial bars
  • The HCS 308 designee named (not the licensee itself) with the Board Resolution where required; board members' HCS 9165 statements signed and kept at the organisation
  • The employee dishonesty bond on HCS 402 obtained — $10,000 minimum including third-party coverage, the State of California named as beneficiary — with the original ready for HCSB and a copy for the administrative file
  • The $1,000,000 per occurrence / $3,000,000 aggregate general and professional liability certificate and the workers' compensation certificate covering affiliated home care aides in hand, each showing policy number, dates, carrier, broker and limits
  • This manual adopted and Exhibit A printed under its cover sheet, with the Section B documents generated from it — B2 job descriptions from, B3 personnel policies from and, B4 training plan from and, B5 program description from, B6 insurance from
  • The B4 training plan naming the trainer, written objectives and the title and duration of both tiers — with dementia content if the application is made on or after 1 January 2027
  • The one-packet rule understood: all Section A forms with original signatures in booklet order, all Section B documents, and the $5,603 cheque or money order payable to "California Department of Social Services" mailed together to M.S. 9-14-90, with a photocopy kept
  • The Guardian agency account planned, the HCO PIN procedure understood, and a Live Scan vendor located for every person to be examined
  • The orientation budgeted ($50.00, non-refundable, virtual, not on demand) and the person who will attend identified
  • The registry gate built into hiring: no affiliated home care aide has direct client contact before the registry shows cleared or exemption on file, and no aide is present with a client before the five entry-level training hours
  • The TB system ready: examinations 90 days before or within 7 days after employment, certificates filed, two-yearly re-examinations diarised, and the two-year transfer-certificate rule understood
  • The three reporting clocks posted where schedulers work: mandated abuse reports by the observing reporter to county APS or law enforcement (adults) or the county child welfare agency or law enforcement (children) — never to CDSS first — with a copy of every report kept for department review; the IMMEDIATE notice to CDSS when an aide leaves; and the aide's own 10-day address-change duty
  • The complaint route named in the client pack: the Agency's own process plus the CCLD Centralized Complaint and Information Bureau
  • The posting plan ready: licence, business hours and any other required information conspicuous and visible to clients and aides from day one, checked monthly
  • The compliance calendar loaded: licence expiry and the 60-day renewal notice window, every aide's registration anniversary, TB and training due dates, insurance and bond expiries, and the 2029 annual-fee switch noted
  • HCS 9201 requested from CDSS and aligned to it line by line when obtained [not read at edition date — verify]
  • The renewal-process PINs 21-06-HCS and 22-03-HCS extracted and read before the first renewal [not read at edition date — verify]
  • LIC 508's status confirmed with HCSB — in the HCS 281 booklet but not on the current Section A chart [verify]

What the regulator reads before you are licensed

California mandates no policy manual — CDSS desk-reviews your Section B documents, then verifies you by random, unannounced inspections where refusal to produce records is cause for revocation.
