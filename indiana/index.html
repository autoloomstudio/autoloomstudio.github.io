How to get a Indiana personal services agency in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to IC 16-27-4 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Indiana, you need a Personal Services Agency (PSA) License from the Indiana Department of Health (IDOH) before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure, branch offices, renewal and notices; scope of services and the "no order" line; the manager, the written designation of designees and the manager's job description; ownership and management disclosure and owner criminal history; compliance documentation and seven-year retention; office hours and 24-hour availability; Department inspections and penalties; policy development and nondiscrimination; charges and price increases; insurance, lift cover and business registration; quality review.

The numbers the rule actually sets

$250 fee, 120-mile branches, 3 business days to apply for the check and 21 to receive it, two-step TB test, 14-day plan signature, 7-day rights statement, 76-to-104-day reviews, 24-hour phone line, 7-year records — exactly as the statute states them.

What is moving in Indiana right now

This manual applies IC 16-27-2 as amended by P.L.171-2025: eleven offence categories with 10- and 5-year look-backs, nurse aide registry findings, and the new duty to report employee convictions — and it follows the Department's Gateway process rather than the 2018 paper forms. Part D tells you exactly what to check and what to change when the statute or the forms move.

The timeline

  1. Set up: register the entity with the Secretary of State (and the assumed business name, and the Certificate of Authority for an out-of-state entity); obtain the EIN letter; secure an Indiana office (records must be kept or accessible there — IC 16-27-4-18); appoint the manager and sign the designation of designees; obtain lifetime criminal history reports dated within three months for the manager, alternate manager(s), officers and owners; obtain insurance, including lift cover if Option B; set office hours and the 24-hour on-call line; adopt your policy manual; build the client packet; recruit, check, test and evaluate the first workers under so that at least one complete file exists. Do not advertise before the licence issues (16-27-4-23).
  2. Apply in the Gateway with the $250 fee, the nine policies and eight documents (seven forms and the manager's job description), the entity and IRS documents and the criminal history reports. The Department issues the licence after receiving a completed application that demonstrates prima facie compliance and the fee (16-27-4-6(e)); processing times are not published. The Department may inspect on site with the initial licence.
  3. Licence issued for one year from the date of issuance — the Department may issue an initial licence for less than a year to stagger expiry dates (16-27-4-6(h)); enter the expiry and the 60-day renewal-opening date .
  4. First clients: service plan before services (signed within 14 days); rights statement within 7 days; criminal history check applied for before the first in-home visit and in any case within 3 business days; first satisfaction review between day 76 and day 104.
  5. After licensure: report changes in ownership, management and officers as they occur on letterhead with criminal history reports — not with the renewal; give 30 days' written notice before closing or selling (16-27-4-6(h)); a new owner obtains its own licence before operating (6(i)); renew in the Gateway as soon as the 60-day window opens and in any case well before the last 15 days (IDOH page; Form 53591); keep the compliance documentation for seven years.
  6. Public programmes: after licensure you may seek enrolment as a Medicaid waiver (FSSA) or Veterans Affairs provider; keep those programmes' policies in a separate binder (Form 53391 Section VIII).

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • Entity registered with the Secretary of State (and assumed name / Certificate of Authority); IRS EIN letter; Indiana office with secure record storage and a telephone answered during stated hours
  • Manager appointed by owner resolution; manager's job description; written designation of alternate manager(s) and on-call designees; organisational chart
  • Ownership and management disclosure complete; lifetime criminal history reports within three months for manager, alternate manager(s), officers and owners, checked against and reviewed against IC 16-27-2-3
  • The nine policies and eight documents (seven forms and the manager's job description) ready to upload; your policy manual adopted; FSSA/waiver policies kept separate
  • Insurance certificates on file (general liability; workers' compensation; motor; lift services cover if Option B)
  • Office hours set and entered in the Gateway; 24-hour on-call line working and tested; on-call rota staffed with named designees
  • At least one complete worker file : check applied for before the first in-home visit, specification checked, result reviewed, TB baseline, written test and skills demonstration dated before any unsupervised attendant care, training record with both signatures, orientation
  • Client packet assembled: service agreement with the schedule of charges and price-increase policy, service plan form, rights statement with all 13 items and the Department's complaint line and business hours completed, office hours notice, emergency sheet
  • Client record system ready; home folder set; trackers for the 14-day, 7-day and 76–104-day deadlines
  • Complaint, referral-notification, declined-assistance, APS and incident forms ready; the manager and designees know the APS and DCS hotlines
  • Compliance file map completed; everything kept or accessible at the Indiana office; the 24-hour production log ready
  • Calendar started: licence expiry and 60-day renewal window; insurance renewals; annual self-audit; TB annual screening per worker
  • Lift services (Option B only): liability policy covering lifts; general training; client-specific training records; manufacturer's manuals on file
  • Marketing and website describe the Agency only as a licensed personal services agency providing non-medical attendant care, homemaker and companion services, and appeared only after the licence issued
