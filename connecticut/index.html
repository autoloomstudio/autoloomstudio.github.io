How to get a Connecticut homemaker-companion agency certificate of registration in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to Conn. Gen. Stat. ch. 400o (sections 20-670 to 20-686) and Regs. Conn. State Agencies · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in Connecticut, you need a Homemaker-Companion Agency (HCA) Certificate of Registration from the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection (DCP) before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Registration as a homemaker-companion agency — the § 20-671 requirement in the statute's own gate sentence, the registration-not-licence framing stated plainly, who is an agency (including registries) and the DPH medical-lane exclusion, with the consequences of operating unregistered stated soberly; the nonmedical scope in the statutory definitions verbatim with the DCP permitted and prohibited lists and the decline-and-refer rule; the application — the Secretary of the State first, the entity-name rule, eLicense or the DCP form, the $375 fee, the owner criminal history records checks and the four sworn certifications that are the compliance skeleton of the whole manual; the annual renewal on the 31 October expiry with the non-receipt-no-excuse rule; certificate display and registered-status advertising with the registration number in every advertisement and the no-endorsement rule; advertising content on the exact nonmedical-care tagline, the no-medical-words rule and the memory-care disclosure condition; ownership changes against the non-transferable certificate with the passive-investor exceptions; the surety bond or insurance at ten thousand dollars minimum with the employee-theft coverage term and the lapse warning; and DCP inspections, investigations and discipline — records open at all reasonable hours, subpoenas and injunctions, the six grounds, notice-and-hearing rights, the three-strikes mandatory revocation and the one-year reinstatement lockout.

The numbers the rule actually sets

The $375 fee and 31 October expiry, the ≥$10,000 bond with the employee-theft coverage term, the 7-day contract clock, the seven boldface disclosures, the 60-day rate notice, the tagline mandate, the badges and the three-strikes rule — exactly as the statute states them.

What is moving in Connecticut right now

Connecticut rebuilt this chapter in three waves — P.A. 23-48 (the tagline mandate, the person-centred contract, three-strikes revocation), P.A. 23-99 (the name disclosure, the 60-day rate notice, the cessation notices) and P.A. 24-39 (the registry duty and, from 1 July 2025, the photo ID badges) — and this edition is written to that amended text throughout, captured 18 November 2026. The ground that could move is tracked honestly: the 2026 training act (HB 5143) passed both chambers in May 2026, but its signature and public act number were NOT confirmed at capture — no sentence of this manual rests on it. Part D carries each watch with what to do, and every summary source is graded "verify", never passed off as read.

The timeline

  1. Decide the service set — companion and homemaker services only, the nonmedical boundary documented: anyone planning medical or nursing work needs the DPH lane instead, and a DPH-licensed operator forms a separate entity to register
  2. Name and form the entity at the Secretary of the State FIRST, with the entity-name rule in mind — no "nurse", "nursing", "health", "healthcare" or "medical" — because DCP requires SOTS registration before the application
  3. Secure the surety bond or insurance policy of not less than $10,000, with coverage including theft by an employee from a person served, before certifying to it
  4. Build the compliance machinery before filing — the contract or service plan template, the comprehensive background check programme and the open-records posture — because the four § 20-672(a) certifications are sworn in the present tense
  5. File via eLicense or the DCP application PDF with the $375 fee, the owner state and national criminal history records checks and the four sworn certifications
  6. Answer every DCP query promptly — the certificate issues on a completed application and fee, and no processing time is published or statutory, so plan the start date with margin
  7. Know the denial lane — ten days to request a hearing after a notice of denial, chapter 54 contested-case procedure, and a one-year wait to reapply if the denial is sustained
  8. On issuance, load the seven-lane compliance calendar and arm the annual 31 October renewal — non-receipt of the renewal notice is no excuse
  9. Run the advertising set from day one — the certificate exhibited on request, registered status and the registration number in every advertisement, the nonmedical-care tagline at the top of every channel, and no endorsement claim ever
  10. Issue the badge stock — name and photograph, worn at every client appointment — and put the client-facing clocks live: the name disclosure before first entry, the signed nonmedical-care notice before services start, the contract within seven calendar days with the printed complaint guide, and the sixty-day rate-notice rule
  11. Run the screening machinery on every worker the § 20-670(6) sweep reaches — independent contractors, pool and temporary staff included — before any offer or contract, with outcomes recorded only in the neutral vocabulary
  12. Hold the inspection posture permanently — all records open at all reasonable hours, the production log current, the three-strikes counter watched, and Part D reviewed at each annual review

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The service set confirmed — companion and homemaker services only, the nonmedical boundary documented, and anyone needing the DPH medical lane pointed there instead
  • All six option switches set with the alternatives deleted, and every bracketed field filled
  • The entity named and formed at the Secretary of the State before the DCP application, with no "nurse", "nursing", "health", "healthcare" or "medical" in the entity name
  • The surety bond or insurance policy of not less than $10,000 bound, with coverage including theft by an employee from a person served, and the renewal tickler set — a lapse falsifies the sworn certification
  • The contract or service plan template complete against every statutory element and every regulation extra, with the completeness checklist ready to run before every signature
  • The comprehensive background check programme running through a PBSA-accredited, FCRA-compliant vendor, completed before any offer or contract, with outcomes recorded only as eligible or not eligible
  • The prospective-employee statement forms and the Form I-9 process in place, with the employment-plus-three-years retention clock set
  • The written client notice of the background-check policy ready for every client
  • The record set organised so any record DCP names can be produced without a search, at all reasonable hours
  • The four sworn certifications true in the present tense before the application is signed
  • The eLicense account created or the current DCP application PDF obtained — DCP wording never invented, placeholders tracked on the forms tracker
  • The completed application filed with the $375 fee and the owner state and national criminal history records checks — no processing time exists, so the start date is planned with margin
  • The denial lane understood — ten days to request a hearing, chapter 54 procedure, one year to reapply if sustained
  • The seven-lane compliance calendar loaded and owned, with the annual 31 October renewal armed — non-receipt of the renewal notice is no excuse
  • The tagline audit run on every channel — the exact nonmedical-care words at the top of every web page, social post, print and audio-visual advertisement — with registered status and the registration number in every advertisement and no endorsement claim ever
  • The printed consumer brochure and the website both live, provided on request
  • The badge stock issued — name and photograph, worn at every client appointment
  • The two pre-service notices live on every intake — the full-legal-name disclosure before the caregiver first enters the home, and the signed nonmedical-care notice before services start
  • The seven-calendar-day contract clock and the printed DCP complaint-guide delivery running on every client, with the sixty-day rate-notice rule armed
  • The registry employment-status notice signed before commencement wherever independent contractors are placed, with the four-day emergency lane on the DCP form
  • The prohibited-clause audit run on every template — no non-compete, no no-hire clause, no agency-held power of attorney
  • The two ten-day cessation notices understood and never merged — the client notice with its four contents, and the agency-wide notice to DCP
  • The three-strikes counter watched, and Part D reviewed — the 2026 training act verified before any reliance, and every watch item logged
