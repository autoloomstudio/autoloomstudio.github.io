How to get a South Carolina in-home care provider license in 2026: requirements, timeline and checklist

Updated October 2026 · Written to S.C. Code Ann. Section 44-70-10 et seq. and Reg. 60-122 · Not legal advice

If you want to open a non-medical home care agency in South Carolina, you need a In-Home Care Provider (IHCP) License from the South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH) — Bureau of Health Facilities Licensing before you take a client. This page sets out what the regulator looks for, in the order you will meet it, and ends with the $1 readiness checklist.

What the license covers

Licensure as an In-Home Care Provider — the § 44-70-30 requirement with the regulation's own operate-and-advertise ban, the cease-operation duty, display, non-transferability and the no-guarantee clause quoted; who does not need the licence, in the statute's own words, with the financially-interested-referral trap stated plainly; the strictly-non-medical service lane with the thirteen-task menu, medication reminders only and the home health and Medicaid-waiver boundaries; the application with form 1307's oath and notarized verification, the signatory SLED check and drug test on their clocks, the evidence set and the substantial-compliance gate; the in-state zoned primary office, multiple locations and the similar-name rule; the annual renewal against the $200 late fee and the thirty-day automatic unlicensed cliff; the $50 amendment lane against the ownership-change new-licence rule and the separate-lines-of-business duty; no-notice inspections, investigations, consultations and the administrator-signed plan of correction; enforcement through the violation classes, the penalty matrix, the reinstatement lanes, appeals and variances; the administrator's responsibility for the overall management and operation; the policy and procedure system that § 301 makes a licensing requirement, with its two-year administrator-signed review; and the insurance, bonding and workers' compensation cover that is both an application exhibit and an ongoing operating duty.

The numbers the rule actually sets

The $1,000 initial and $800 annual fees, the $200 flat late fee and the 30-day automatic-unlicensed cliff, the 90-day SLED and 30-day drug-test clocks, the 24-hour dual-recipient incident report with its separate 5-day investigation report, and the $100–$5,000 penalty matrix — exactly as the regulation states them.

What is moving in South Carolina right now

South Carolina rewrote its in-home care regulation: the 2014 DHEC-era Reg. 61-122 became Reg. 60-122 through the 2025 and 2026 State Register actions, growing from eight sections to eleven — adding the § 301 policies-and-procedures mandate, insurance as an operating duty, client records, the 13-task care-services menu, infection control, client rights and disaster preparedness. DHEC itself was dissolved in 2024; DPH licenses now. Competitor templates still citing "61-122" or "DHEC" are citing dead law — this manual never does, and carries the old regulation only on F-16, the transition map. The timeline

  1. Confirm the business is in the licence — for compensation providing or arranging non-medical in-home care, including through financially-interested referrals — and that none of the § 44-70-20(3) exclusions covers you; document the answer
  2. Stay in the non-medical lane: the thirteen § 700 tasks, medication reminders only — anything skilled or medical is home health under § 44-69-10 et seq., which your policy manual does not supply
  3. Hold the marketing — § 103.A reaches representing, advertising and marketing, so the website and the advertisements wait until the licence issues
  4. Form the entity with the Secretary of State and assemble the ownership papers, with the provider name cleared of DPH's similar-name test — a franchise adds its geographic area to the name
  5. Secure the in-state primary office in a commercially zoned or unzoned area with the zoning permit or county/municipal letter, and register any multiple location with DPH before representing it
  6. Bind the insurance and keep it for the full licence period — liability cover or surety bond at $100,000 per occurrence / $300,000 aggregate, indemnity coverage, and workers' compensation per § 42-5-10 et seq.
  7. Finish your policy manual first — the § 301 policies and procedures addressing each section of the regulation, with the random drug-testing policy finished as the § 103.F.5 application exhibit
  8. Run the signatory screening on its clocks — the SLED name-based check not older than 90 calendar days at DPH receipt, and the five-panel drug test not earlier than 30 days before receipt, documented on the chain with outcomes in the neutral vocabulary only
  9. Complete form 1307 on the current edition — the oath, the owner or two-officer signatures, and the sworn, notarised Part D verification — with the full attachment set assembled
  10. Pay the $1,000 initial fee and attach the proof — all outstanding fees and finally assessed penalties must be paid before issuance — then file, email preferred
  11. Demonstrate substantial compliance — no published processing time exists and your policy manual prints none; stand ready for inspection without prior notice from the first day
  12. Day one — post the licence or keep it readily available to the public, load the six-lane calendar, arm the renewal lane against the 30-day unlicensed cliff, run the three screening lanes and the incident system, and start the two-year review cycle on the policy review log

The readiness checklist

Tick every one of these before you apply and before any inspection:

  • The lane confirmed — non-medical in-home care, strictly limited to the § 700 task menu — and the exclusion question answered and documented: home health agencies and hospices, house-cleaning-only agencies, direct hires by the client or family, and uncompensated 501(c)(3) religious care need no IHCP licence at all
  • No operating, representing, advertising or marketing as an in-home care provider before the licence issues — the website and the advertisements wait
  • All six option switches set with the alternatives deleted, and every bracketed field filled
  • The entity formed with the Secretary of State, with the SOS documentation, officer lists, owners-of-5%-or-more lists and LLC member lists assembled for the application
  • The provider name cleared of DPH's similar-name test — and, for a franchise, the geographic area included in the name
  • The in-state primary office secured in a commercially zoned or unzoned area, with the zoning permit or the county or municipal letter on file
  • Any multiple location registered by application and approved by DPH before it is established, operated or represented
  • The insurance bound and kept for the full licence period — liability cover or a surety bond at $100,000 per occurrence / $300,000 aggregate, indemnity coverage, and workers' compensation per § 42-5-10 et seq.
  • The administrator designated — responsible for the overall management and operation of the provider
  • This manual completed into the § 301 document — every placeholder filled, written policies and procedures addressing each section of Reg. 60-122, accessible to staff in print or electronically at all times
  • The random drug-testing policies and procedures finished — they file with the application as the § 103.F.5 exhibit
  • Every form 1307 signatory's SLED name-based check completed, with the evidence not older than 90 calendar days at DPH's receipt of the application
  • Every signatory's five-panel drug test taken not earlier than 30 days before DPH's receipt, the documentation chain complete, and the outcome recorded only as eligible or not eligible
  • Form 1307 completed on the current edition, signed by the owner(s) or by two corporate officers, with the Part D verification sworn and notarised
  • The $1,000 initial fee paid and the proof of payment attached — all outstanding fees and finally assessed penalties cleared before issuance
  • The application filed — email to IHCP@dph.sc.gov preferred — and every DPH request for additional information answered promptly
  • Substantial compliance demonstrable on the day DPH looks — no published processing time exists, so plan on the evidence, not on a schedule
  • The no-notice inspection posture standing from day one — records readily accessible, the expected-staff-return information ready, and the administrator-signed plan-of-correction drill understood
  • The screening machinery running on every hire: the before-employment criminal background check, the pre-employment five-panel test on its 30-day-before-offer clock, and the random programme live for signatories and caregivers alike
  • The service documents ready: the service agreement and the written rights and assurances signed before provision of in-home care, and the care services plan completed within seven business days of first service
  • The incident system ready — the 24-hour or next-business-day report to BOTH the responsible party and DPH's electronic reporting system, and the separate five-calendar-day written investigation report, on two clocks never merged
  • The renewal lane armed on day one — the $800 annual renewal diarised, with the $200 late fee and the 30-day automatic-unlicensed cliff in view
  • The two-year policy review cycle started on the policy review log with the administrator's signature, and the Part D watch items logged on the law watch log
